## Measurements

P is the worker count, k is points per worker, Ts is direct serial time, and Tp is startup-inclusive parallel time for the same P × k points. I averaged the paired speedup ratios, not the timing means.

```text
Speedup S = Ts / Tp
Scaled efficiency E = S / P
Standard error SE = SD / √5
```

## Least-squares derivation

I fitted the mean speedup y at each P by minimizing the unweighted squared errors. Here Σ means summing over the 12 worker counts.

```text
Q(α) = Σ [y − P + α × (P − 1)]²
dQ/dα = 2 × Σ [(P − 1) × (y − P + α × (P − 1))] = 0
α̂ = Σ [(P − 1) × (P − y)] / Σ [(P − 1)²]
```

I obtained **α̂ = 0.784991225892** and:

```text
Sfit(P) = 0.215008774108 × P + 0.784991225892
RMSE = √(sum of squared residuals / number of worker counts)
```

RMSE is **0.246365** and descriptive R² is **0.905843**. The residual-based standard error of α̂ is **0.011439** (n − 1 degrees of freedom). This assumes independent, equal-variance errors; it is not a calibrated confidence interval for these variable measurements.

An unrestricted line gives S = 0.221494 × P + 0.728783; I kept the required Gustafson fit.

## Conclusion

The fitted α̂ is an **effective overhead parameter**, not an instrumented serial-code fraction. I did not clamp its estimate to [0, 1]. The model forces S(1) = 1, but the measured one-worker path includes spawn overhead. Its residual is -0.472168; it affects fit diagnostics but not α̂ because P − 1 = 0.

Runtime increased rather than remaining constant. I retained the noisy P = 6 trials and did not attribute the variation to unmeasured thermal or NUMA effects.
